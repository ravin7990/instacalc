What is Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)?

The Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) is the mean annual growth rate of an investment over a specified period of time longer than one year. It represents the geometric progression ratio that provides a constant rate of return, smoothing out market volatility and showing what an investment yielded on an annually compounded basis.


The Mathematical CAGR Formula

The standard formula is: CAGR = (Ending Value / Beginning Value)^(1 / Years) - 1.

What is CAGR and how is it calculated?

CAGR measures the constant rate at which an investment grows annually over a specified time period, assuming the gains compound. It is calculated as: CAGR = (Ending Value / Beginning Value)^(1 / Years) - 1.

What is the difference between CAGR and Absolute Return?

Absolute return measures total gain or loss without considering time duration. CAGR converts this growth into an annualized average rate, accounting for the effect of compounding over years.

Can I use CAGR for Systematic Investment Plans (SIP)?

No, CAGR is mathematically designed for single point-to-point lump sum investments. For staggered periodic cash flows like monthly SIPs, you must use XIRR (Extended Internal Rate of Return).
