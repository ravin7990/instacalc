How the FD Calculator Computes Quarterly Compounding Interest

A Fixed Deposit (FD) is a secure, guaranteed-return investment where capital is locked with a bank or post office for a fixed tenure. In Indian commercial banks, fixed deposit interest is compounded quarterly using the standard formula: A = P * (1 + r/4)^(4*t). Our FD calculator models both cumulative reinvestment FDs and non-cumulative periodic monthly or quarterly payout options.

Senior Citizen Preferential Rates & High-Yield Bank Tenures

Indian banks (including SBI, HDFC, ICICI, and PNB) offer senior citizens (aged 60 and above) an additional interest rate of 0.50% (50 basis points) above standard public rates. Several lenders also run special super senior citizen schemes (aged 80+) providing up to 0.75% to 0.80% extra yield on select tenures between 400 days and 5 years.

Taxation of Fixed Deposits: TDS Rules, Form 15G/15H & Section 80TTB

FD interest is fully taxable as 'Income from Other Sources' at your marginal income tax slab rate. Banks deduct 10% TDS if annual interest exceeds ₹40,000 for regular individuals or ₹50,000 for senior citizens. Senior citizens can claim up to ₹50,000 in interest deductions under Section 80TTB. Individuals with zero taxable income can submit Form 15G (or Form 15H for seniors) to avoid TDS deductions.

Sovereign Safety & DICGC ₹5 Lakh Insurance Protection

Bank fixed deposits in all registered commercial banks, small finance banks, and cooperative banks are insured up to ₹5,00,000 (₹5 Lakh) per depositor per bank by the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC), a wholly owned subsidiary of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). This insurance covers both principal and accumulated interest.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is fixed deposit (FD) interest compounded in Indian banks?

In most commercial and public sector Indian banks, fixed deposit interest is compounded on a quarterly basis using the formula: A = P * (1 + r/4)^(4*t). Here P is the principal deposit, r is the annual interest rate, and t is the investment duration in years.

What additional interest rate do senior citizens receive on bank FDs?

Senior citizens (aged 60 and above) typically receive an additional interest rate of 0.50% (50 basis points) across most tenures in Indian banks. Several banks offer special super senior citizen schemes (aged 80+) providing up to 0.75% to 0.80% extra yield.

How is fixed deposit interest taxed under Indian income tax laws?

FD interest is fully taxable at your applicable income tax slab rate under 'Income from Other Sources'. Banks deduct 10% TDS if annual interest exceeds ₹40,000 for regular individuals or ₹50,000 for senior citizens (under Section 80TTB). Submit Form 15G/15H if your total income is below the taxable threshold.

Is my bank fixed deposit safe and insured in India?

Yes. Bank deposits in all commercial, regional rural, and cooperative banks are insured up to ₹5,00,000 per depositor per bank by the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC), a wholly owned subsidiary of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI).
