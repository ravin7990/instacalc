Understanding ISO Week Numbers & Duration Math

The ISO 8601 week-numbering system is the internationally recognized benchmark used across manufacturing, enterprise resource planning (ERP), corporate finance, and sprint management. It standardizes calendar weeks by designating Monday as the starting day and assigning Week 1 to the week containing the year's first Thursday.


Adding and Subtracting Weeks: Planning project roadmaps or pregnancy trimesters requires shifting forward or backward by an exact multiple of 7 days. Our tool computes exact target calendar dates, accounting for month-end rollovers and leap years automatically.


Key Metrics Provided: Current ISO week number, percentage of the year elapsed, remaining weeks until year-end, financial quarter (Q1–Q4), and exact days between milestones.

What is an ISO week date?

An ISO week date identifies dates by year, week number (W01–W53), and weekday (1 for Monday to 7 for Sunday). For example, 2026-W36-1 represents Monday of Week 36 in 2026.

Can a date in January belong to the previous year's week?

Yes. If January 1 falls on a Friday, Saturday, or Sunday, those days belong to Week 52 or 53 of the preceding year because the week contains fewer than four days of the new year.

How many weeks are in 90 days?

90 days is equal to 12 weeks and 6 days (or approximately 12.86 decimal weeks).

How do project managers use week numbers?

Software teams and global supply chains use week numbers (e.g., "Feature release scheduled for Week 42") to coordinate timelines without ambiguity across differing international holiday schedules.
