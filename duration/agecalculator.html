How to Calculate Age from Date of Birth: Formula & Rules

To calculate age from date of birth manually, you must follow the Borrowing Subtraction Rule. The core formula is:

Age = Target Date - Date of Birth

Follow these manual steps to calculate your age using pen and paper:

  1. Align Dates: Write down the Target Date (Year, Month, Day) on top, and your Date of Birth (Year, Month, Day) below it.
  2. Subtract Days: If the Target Day is smaller than the Birth Day, borrow 1 month from the Target Month column. Add the number of days in the previous month (e.g., 30 or 31) to the Target Day, then subtract.
  3. Subtract Months: Subtract the months. If the Target Month is now smaller than the Birth Month, borrow 1 year (12 months) from the Target Year column. Add 12 to the Target Month, then subtract.
  4. Subtract Years: Subtract the years to get the final year count.

Example: How to Calculate Age with Numbers

Let's run a practical example of manual age calculation using the borrow method. Suppose we want to calculate the age of an individual born on 25 November 1998 as of 15 May 2026.

Step 1: Set up the columns:
Target Date: 2026 Year, 05 Month, 15 Day
Birth Date: 1998 Year, 11 Month, 25 Day

Step 2: Subtract the Days column (15 - 25):
Since 15 is smaller than 25, we borrow 1 month from the Month column. May (Month 05) becomes April (Month 04). April has 30 days. We add 30 to our 15 days, making it 45 days.
Now: 45 days - 25 days = 20 days.

Step 3: Subtract the Months column (04 - 11):
Since our Target Month is now 04, and 04 is smaller than 11, we borrow 1 year from the Year column. 2026 becomes 2025. We add 12 months to our 4 months, making it 16 months.
Now: 16 months - 11 months = 5 months.

Step 4: Subtract the Years column (2025 - 1998):
Now: 2025 - 1998 = 27 years.

Final Calculated Age: 27 Years, 5 Months, and 20 Days.

Common Mistakes When Calculating Age

When calculating chronological age manually or setting inputs, avoid these frequent errors:

  • Confusing the Cutoff Date: Many candidates mistakenly compute their age as of the *application submission date* rather than the official *exam cutoff date* specified in the government notification.
  • Assuming a Fixed 30-Day Month: Approximating that every month has 30 days leads to errors of 1 to 2 days over a year. Our calculator dynamically tracks whether a month has 28, 29, 30, or 31 days.
  • Ignoring Leap Years: Leap years (adding February 29) occur every four years. Over 20 years, ignoring leap years introduces a 5-day error in your chronological age calculation.
  • Incorrect Date Format: Make sure you do not transpose the day and month (e.g., confusing 05/09 as 5 September in DD/MM format vs 9 May in MM/DD format).
