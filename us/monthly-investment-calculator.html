Historical S&P 500 Returns & Compounding Mechanics

Over the past 50+ years, broad US market index funds such as the S&P 500 and total stock market index funds have generated an annualized nominal return of approximately 10% (or ~7% adjusted for inflation with dividends reinvested). Consistent monthly investing captures dollar-cost averaging advantages: buying more shares when market prices drop and fewer shares when prices rise.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)?

Dollar-Cost Averaging is an investment strategy where you invest a fixed dollar amount at regular intervals regardless of share prices, reducing the risk of bad market timing.

What is the historical average annual return of the S&P 500?

Historically, the S&P 500 has averaged approximately 10% annualized nominal returns (around 7% after adjusting for inflation with dividends reinvested) over multi-decade periods.

How does dividend reinvestment (DRIP) impact long-term portfolio returns?

Reinvesting dividends automatically purchases additional shares of stock or index funds, compounding returns significantly faster than taking cash dividends.
