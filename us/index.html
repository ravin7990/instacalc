Frequently asked questions

Are all the US calculators free to use?

Yes. Every calculator on the hub is completely free, requires no sign-up, and runs entirely in your browser — nothing is uploaded to a server.

Does the 401(k) calculator include employer matching?

Yes. It models your contribution rate, employer match formulas, salary growth, and the current IRS annual contribution limits to project your retirement nest egg.

How accurate is the US Mortgage Calculator?

It estimates monthly PITI (principal, interest, property taxes, insurance, PMI) using your inputs. Actual payments depend on your lender's escrow terms, so treat the result as a planning estimate.

Are business days calculated with US federal holidays?

Yes. The Days & Business Days calculator excludes weekends and the official US federal holiday calendar when counting working days.

Can I use these tools on my phone?

Absolutely — every tool is mobile-first, with touch-friendly inputs, range sliders, and responsive layouts designed for small screens.

Is my data saved anywhere?

No. All calculations run locally in your browser. Nothing you type is transmitted or stored, and clearing the page resets everything.

Which states does the Sales Tax Calculator cover?

All 50 states plus DC, using combined state and average local rates. For exact rates in special districts, verify with your state's revenue department.

How does the FIRE calculator work?

It applies the 4% rule — annual expenses multiplied by 25 — to find your financial independence number, then calculates years to reach it at your savings rate and expected return.

Do I need to account for inflation?

For long-horizon goals, yes. Run projections at a conservative real-return rate (about 7% instead of 10%) to approximate inflation-adjusted growth.

Are these tools financial advice?

No. All tools are for educational purposes only and are not financial, tax, or legal advice. Verify rates and rules with the IRS, your state revenue agency, or a licensed professional before acting.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting that sales tax varies by state — use the state selector, not a national guess.
  • Mixing pre-tax 401(k) and after-tax Roth amounts in the same projection.
  • Using nominal historical returns without checking a conservative real-return scenario.
