The Retirement Calculator pulls together Social Security, 401(k)/IRA savings, pension, and spending assumptions into a single retirement-income picture — does your nest egg actually last?

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter your current age, planned retirement age, and expected lifespan (85 is a conservative planning default).
  2. Enter your current retirement savings across all accounts.
  3. Enter your monthly or annual savings rate (401k + IRA + brokerage).
  4. Estimate your expected Social Security benefit — the SSA "my Social Security" portal gives your actual projection.
  5. Enter your expected annual spending in retirement (70-80% of pre-retirement spending is the common planning rule).
  6. Click Calculate — you'll see projected savings at retirement, the Social Security contribution, and whether savings last through your lifespan.

The method behind the math

Nest egg at retirement uses annual compounding of contributions and returns. Retirement drawdown subtracts annual spending while continuing to earn returns on the remaining balance — a "Monte Carlo"-style deterministic model with a single return rate. Success = savings + Social Security income lasting until your assumed lifespan.

Worked example

A 35-year-old with $75,000 saved, contributing $1,200/month until age 67 at a 7% return, reaches a nest egg of about $1.67 million. At $65,000/year spending plus $28,000 Social Security, annual withdrawal need is only ~$37,000 — a 2.2% withdrawal rate, comfortably below the 4% rule. Retirement is fully funded.

Rules and regulations that affect your result

  • Full Social Security retirement age is 67 for those born 1960+; claiming at 62 permanently reduces benefits by ~30%.
  • Delayed claiming past 67 adds 8% per year up to age 70 — one of the best guaranteed-return deals in US finance.
  • Social Security benefits are taxed at up to 85% for higher-income retires.
  • Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) from traditional accounts begin at age 73.
  • Medicare starts at 65 — budget ~$185/month Part B premiums (2026 estimate) plus supplemental plans.

Practical tips

  • Use the SSA calculator for your personalized Social Security estimate rather than rules of thumb.
  • Healthcare before Medicare (62-64) is the biggest "invisible" cost — COBRA or ACA marketplace plans can run $1,200+/month.
  • Delay Social Security to 70 if you can — the 8%/year bump is a guaranteed inflation-adjusted annuity.
  • Convert traditional IRA funds to Roth in low-income years between retirement and RMD age.
  • Keep 2-3 years of spending in cash/bonds during retirement to avoid selling stocks in downturns.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming you'll spend less — most retires spend their pre-retirement amount for the first 5-10 years.
  • Ignoring inflation — $65,000 spending at 3% inflation becomes $118,000 in 20 years.
  • Forgetting taxes — traditional 401(k)/IRA withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income.
  • Counting Social Security at "current" benefit levels without adjusting for future COLA.
  • Underestimating lifespan — a healthy 65-year-old man has a ~50% chance of living to 85+.

Frequently asked questions

How much do I need to retire?

The 25-times rule: annual spending × 25. Spending 60,000 dollars a year suggests a 1.5 million dollar nest egg, adjusted for inflation (to this year's dollars).

When should I claim Social Security?

Claiming at 62 permanently reduces benefits; full retirement age is 67; delaying past 67 adds 8 percent per year up to age 70. For higher earners, delaying is often one of the best guaranteed-return deals available.

How is Social Security calculated?

Based on your highest 35 years of indexed earnings. Zero-earning years count as zeros, which is why career gaps, especially early, permanently reduce the benefit.

Is Social Security taxed?

Up to 85 percent of benefits can be taxable for higher-income retirees; some states also tax benefits. Plan with your after-tax benefit, not the gross number.

What should my retirement withdrawal rate be?

The classic 4 percent rule; more conservative modern planning uses 3.0 to 3.5 percent, reflecting lower forecast returns and longer lifespans.

When should I start Medicare?

At 65. Missing the 7-month initial enrollment window triggers permanent late penalties (Part B up to 10 percent per year missed). COBRA alone doesn't satisfy this window — enroll by 65 even if still working.

How much should healthcare before Medicare cost?

Retirees aged 62-64 without employer coverage can expect 800 to 2,000 dollars per month for ACA-compliant plans, varying widely by subsidy eligibility.

What is a Roth conversion ladder?

Converting portions of traditional IRA/401(k) to Roth over several low-income years creates a tax-free withdrawal pipeline accessible penalty-free after 5 years — the standard bridge for early retirees.

How long does my retirement savings need to last?

A healthy 65-year-old man has roughly a 50 percent chance of reaching 85; a woman about 55 percent. Plan to age 95 for a conservative margin.

What is sequence-of-returns risk?

A bear market immediately before or after retirement can permanently reduce safe withdrawal capacity. Mitigation: a cash or bond reserve covering 2-3 years of spending.
