CD Calculator for savers: if you are comparing a certificate of deposit against a savings account or Treasury bill, this tool projects exactly what your deposit earns at a fixed rate. Enter your deposit amount, the bank's APY, and the term length below, and the calculator shows your maturity value, total interest, and the effective yield after early-withdrawal penalties — FDIC-insured up to $250,000 per depositor per institution.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter the deposit amount (typically $500 minimum, up to FDIC-insured $250,000 per depositor per institution).
  2. Enter the annual percentage yield (APY) the bank quotes.
  3. Choose the term (3 months to 10 years — 12-month and 18-month are the most common).
  4. Pick the compounding frequency (daily is most common; the APY already includes it).
  5. Click Calculate — you'll see ending balance, total interest earned, and monthly-equivalent interest.

The method behind the math

A = P(1 + APY)^t, where P is the deposit, APY the annual percentage yield, and t the term in years. APY already reflects compounding, so no further adjustments are needed. A $25,000 CD at 4.35% APY for 12 months grows to $26,087.50 — $1,087.50 in guaranteed interest.

Worked example

$25,000 in a 12-month CD at 4.35% APY earns $1,087.50. The same money in a 5-year CD at 4.0% earns about $5,416 — but you lose access for 5 years. The "ladder" strategy (splitting across 1-, 2-, 3-, 4-, and 5-year terms) gives you both yield and liquidity.

Rules and regulations that affect your result

  • FDIC insurance covers up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category (individual/joint/trust).
  • Early-withdrawal penalties are severe — typically 3-6 months of interest for short-term CDs, 12+ months for longer terms.
  • CDs are NOT liquid — the money is locked until maturity.
  • Interest earned is taxable as ordinary income in the year earned (except within an IRA-CD).
  • CD rates track the Fed funds rate — when the Fed cuts, new CD rates follow lower within weeks.

Practical tips

  • Use a "CD ladder" to balance yield and liquidity — one CD maturing each year.
  • Shop across banks — online banks routinely pay 1 percentage point more than major brick-and-mortar banks.
  • Check FDIC insurance limits before depositing — amounts above $250,000 can be split across banks.
  • Brokered CDs (through Fidelity/Schwab) offer longer terms and sometimes better rates, but carry liquidity and call risk.
  • Watch for promotional "bonus CD" rates that require a linked checking account.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Locking money you might need — CDs are not for emergency funds.
  • Cashing out early and eating the penalty — sometimes it's cheaper to take a personal loan.
  • Not comparing APY (which includes compounding) across banks.
  • Ignoring the maturity date — many banks auto-renew into a lower-rate CD unless you act in the grace period (typically 7-10 days).
  • Depositing over FDIC limits at a single bank.

Frequently asked questions

How much FDIC insurance do I get?

Up to 250,000 dollars per depositor, per bank, per ownership category (individual, joint, trust, retirement). Amounts above can be split across banks or capitalized as brokered deposits.

What happens if I withdraw early?

penalties: typically 3 to 6 months of interest for short-term CDs, 12+ months for longer terms. In some cases, the penalty can exceed the interest earned and eat into principal.

What is the best CD rate right now?

Rates change weekly with the Fed funds rate. Shop online banks and credit unions — they routinely pay 0.75 to 1.5 percentage points more than major brick-and-mortar banks.

Are CDs safe?

Yes — FDIC-insured up to the limit at member banks (NCUA-insured at credit unions). The principal is guaranteed by the government, unlike stocks or bond funds.

Should I ladder CDs?

Usually yes. A ladder (1, 2, 3, 4, 5-year tranches) balances higher long-term rates with annual liquidity, smoothing reinvestment risk as rates change.

Are CD interest earnings taxable?

Yes — as ordinary income in the year earned, even if the CD has not matured.1099-INT is issued each January.

What is an IRA CD?

A CD held inside an IRA wrapper — the interest accrues tax-deferred (traditional) or tax-free (Roth). FDIC-insured and very conservative, though growth is limited.

What is a jumbo CD?

Typically 100,000 dollars or more. Rates can be slightly better but the amount exceeds standard FDIC coverage at a single bank — check limits before depositing.

What is a no-penalty CD?

A CD with a single withdrawal permitted before maturity without penalty, in exchange for a slightly lower rate. Trades yield for liquidity.

What happens at CD maturity?

Most banks auto-renew into the then-current rate for the same term unless you act. A 7-10 day grace period lets you withdraw, add, or change terms without penalty — mark the date.
