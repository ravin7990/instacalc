How to Use the Certificate of Deposit (CD) Calculator

Enter your initial CD deposit principal (e.g., $10,000), the annual percentage yield (APY) offered by your bank or credit union (e.g., 4.5% to 5.25%), the CD term in months or years (e.g., 6-month, 12-month, 3-year, or 5-year), and compounding frequency (daily or monthly). If you anticipate liquidating the CD before maturity, toggle the early withdrawal penalty option to estimate penalty fees (typically 3 to 6 months of interest).

Understanding CD APY, Compounding & FDIC Insurance

Certificates of Deposit offer guaranteed, fixed-rate interest backed by FDIC (for banks) or NCUA (for credit unions) insurance up to $250,000 per depositor per institution. Annual Percentage Yield (APY) reflects the true annual return taking into account compounding interest, whereas the Annual Percentage Rate (APR) is the simple base rate. A CD laddering strategy—splitting money across 6-month, 1-year, 2-year, and 3-year terms—provides ongoing liquidity while capturing top yield.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between APY and interest rate on a CD?

APY (Annual Percentage Yield) accounts for the effect of compound interest throughout the year, whereas the nominal interest rate does not. Therefore, APY is slightly higher than the nominal rate and represents your true annual return.

Are CDs safe from market loss?

Yes, CDs opened at FDIC-insured banks or NCUA-insured credit unions are protected up to $250,000 per depositor, making principal loss virtually impossible within federal limits.

What happens if I withdraw money from a CD before maturity?

Early withdrawals typically trigger an early withdrawal penalty, which usually forfeits a set number of months of interest (e.g., 90 to 180 days of interest on terms under 2 years).
