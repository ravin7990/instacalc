US Time Zones Breakdown (EST, CST, MST, PST) & Daylight Saving Rules

The contiguous United States spans four primary time zones: Eastern Time (UTC-5 / UTC-4 DST), Central Time (UTC-6 / UTC-5 DST), Mountain Time (UTC-7 / UTC-6 DST), and Pacific Time (UTC-8 / UTC-7 DST). Daylight Saving Time (DST) begins on the second Sunday in March and ends on the first Sunday in November. Most of Arizona (except the Navajo Nation) and Hawaii do not observe Daylight Saving Time and remain on standard time year-round.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the time difference between Eastern Time (ET) and Pacific Time (PT)?

Eastern Time is exactly 3 hours ahead of Pacific Time (e.g., 12:00 PM EST is 9:00 AM PST).

Which US states do not observe Daylight Saving Time?

Hawaii and most of Arizona (excluding the Navajo Nation territory) do not observe Daylight Saving Time and maintain standard time throughout the entire year.

What are the standard UTC offsets for US time zones?

Standard offsets: Eastern is UTC-5 (UTC-4 in DST), Central is UTC-6 (UTC-5 in DST), Mountain is UTC-7 (UTC-6 in DST), Pacific is UTC-8 (UTC-7 in DST), Alaska is UTC-9 (UTC-8 in DST), and Hawaii is UTC-10 year-round.
