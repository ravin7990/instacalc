2026 Roth IRA Income Limits & 5-Year Rule Requirements

Contributions to a Roth IRA are made with after-tax dollars, allowing all future earnings and withdrawals in retirement (after age 59½) to be 100% tax-free. To contribute directly, your Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) must fall below IRS phase-out thresholds ($146,000-$161,000 for single filers; $230,000-$240,000 for married filing jointly). You can withdraw your original contributions at any time without taxes or penalties.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 2026 Roth IRA contribution limit?

The IRS annual contribution limit for a Roth IRA is $7,000 for individuals under age 50, with an additional $1,000 catch-up contribution for individuals aged 50 and older (totaling $8,000).

Can I withdraw money from my Roth IRA before age 59½ without penalty?

You can withdraw your original contributions from a Roth IRA at any time, for any reason, with zero taxes and zero penalties. Only withdrawing earnings before age 59½ may incur penalties.

What is the Roth IRA 5-year rule?

The 5-year rule requires that at least 5 tax years have passed since your very first contribution to any Roth IRA before earnings can be withdrawn completely tax-free after age 59½.
