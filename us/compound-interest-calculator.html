How to Use the US Compound Interest Calculator

Enter your starting principal, ongoing additional contributions (monthly or annual), investment duration in years, expected annual interest rate, and compounding frequency (daily, monthly, or annually). The calculator generates a dynamic growth curve, breakdown of principal vs total interest, and year-by-year amortization schedule to help you visualize the exponential acceleration of compound interest.

Compound Interest Formula & The Rule of 72

Compound interest is calculated using the formula: A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) + PMT * [((1 + r/n)^(nt) - 1) / (r/n)], where A is future value, P is principal, r is annual rate, n is compounding periods per year, and t is years. A helpful mental math shortcut is the Rule of 72: divide 72 by your expected annual rate of return to estimate how many years it takes for your investment to double (e.g., at 8% return, money doubles every 9 years).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is interest calculated on both the initial principal and the accumulated interest from previous periods, allowing investments to grow exponentially over time.

How does compounding frequency impact investment return?

More frequent compounding (such as daily or monthly instead of annually) generates slightly higher total yield because interest starts earning returns sooner.

What is the Rule of 72 in finance?

The Rule of 72 is a quick estimation formula: divide 72 by the annual rate of return to calculate the approximate number of years needed to double your invested capital.
