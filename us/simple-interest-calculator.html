Simple Interest Formula, Loans & Promissory Notes Guide

The InstaCalc Simple Interest Calculator calculates the total interest accrued, future balance, and periodic payments for short-term loans, promissory notes, auto loans, and peer-to-peer lending agreements. Unlike compound interest, which charges interest upon previously accumulated interest, simple interest is calculated strictly on the initial principal balance throughout the life of the loan. This tool provides instant transparency for borrowers and lenders managing fixed-rate agreements.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter Loan Principal (P): Input the initial borrowed or invested dollar amount.
  2. Annual Interest Rate (r): Enter the stated annual interest rate percentage.
  3. Time Term (t): Enter the loan duration in years, months, or days.
  4. Review Breakdown: Instantly view total interest owed, total repayment balance, and effective monthly payment schedule.

The Standard Simple Interest Formula

Simple interest is computed using the classical linear financial formula:

I = P × r × t

Total Repayment Amount (A) = P + I = P × (1 + r × t)

Where:

  • I: Total interest accrued over the loan duration.
  • P: Principal starting loan amount.
  • r: Annual interest rate expressed in decimal form (e.g., 6.5% = 0.065).
  • t: Time elapsed in years (if term is in months, $t = \text{months} / 12$; if in days, $t = \text{days} / 365$).

Worked Example: 3-Year Personal Promissory Note

Suppose you lend a colleague $10,000.00 under a formal family promissory note at a 6.0% simple annual interest rate over a 3-year term ($t=3$):

  • Principal (P): $10,000.00
  • Annual Interest Rate (r): 0.06
  • Calculated Interest: $I = $10,000 \times 0.06 \times 3 = $ **$1,800.00**
  • Total Repayment Balance (A): $10,000 + $1,800 = $ **$11,800.00**
  • Equivalent Monthly Payment (36 months): $11,800 / 36 = $ **$327.78/mo**

Simple Interest vs Compound Interest in US Lending

  • Auto Loans: Most traditional US auto loans are daily simple interest loans. Every payment you make first covers the interest that accrued since your last payment; the remaining balance immediately reduces the principal. Paying extra early reduces total interest.
  • Precomputed / Add-On Interest Warning: Some subprime lenders use "add-on" simple interest where all future interest is locked into the loan balance upfront. Paying early under an add-on loan provides zero interest savings.
  • Short-Term Promissory Notes: Simple interest is the standard legal mechanism for promissory notes, private bridge loans, and bond coupons.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Treating the Rate as a Monthly Rate: Stated APR is an annual rate; entering 6% as a monthly rate charges 72% per year.
  • Confusing Simple Interest with Credit Cards: Credit cards compound interest daily; simple interest calculations significantly underestimate credit card revolving debt costs.
  • Neglecting Leap Years in Day-Count Loans: Bank agreements using the 360-day "commercial year" (ordinary interest) vs 365-day (exact interest) result in slight variance.

Frequently asked questions

What is the simple interest formula?

Interest = Principal × Rate × Time. For 10,000 dollars at 8 percent for 3 years: 10,000 × 0.08 × 3 = 2,400 dollars. Total repayment is principal plus interest.

How does simple interest differ from compound interest?

Simple interest calculates only on the original principal. Compound interest adds earned interest to the base, so the interest accrues on interest. Over long terms the difference is substantial.

Where do you see simple interest in the US?

Auto loans (accruing daily on the unpaid balance), short-term personal loans, some private promissory notes, and many bond coupons.

Is simple interest better for borrowers?

Yes, for the same APR and term — no "interest on interest" effect. Prepaying a simple-interest loan immediately stops further interest on the prepaid principal.

What is 365/360 interest accrual?

A common commercial-loan convention where daily interest is calculated with a 360-day year denominator though 365 actual days pass. This slightly raises the effective rate — read the loan note carefully.

How much interest on 20,000 dollars at 6 percent for 5 years?

20,000 × 0.06 × 5 = 6,000 dollars. Total repayment 26,000 dollars — a monthly equal-amortization payment of 433.33 dollars over 60 months.

Can I prepay a simple-interest loan?

Yes—usually with no penalty on personal loans. The interest stops accruing on the prepaid portion the moment it is paid, unlike some precomputed-interest auto loans.

What is the Rule of 78s?

An older precomputed-loan convention that front-loads interest heavily, penalizing early payoff. Banned on many consumer loans since 1993 but still seen in some subprime lending.

What is an AFR (Applicable Federal Rate)?

The IRS minimum interest rate for below-market loans, set monthly. Family loans above the annual gift exclusion must charge at least AFR to avoid gift-tax implications.

Does simple interest mean no fees?

No — origination fees, late fees, and prepayment penalties are separate charges. The APR (Annual Percentage Rate) reflects total cost including most fees.
