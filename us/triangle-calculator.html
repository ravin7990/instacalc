Core Formulas & Trigonometric Theorems

1. Right Triangle (Pythagorean Theorem & Trigonometric Ratios):

c = √(a² + b²),   ∠A = arctan(a ÷ b),   ∠B = 90° − ∠A

Area = ½ × a × b

2. SSS: Heron's Formula (Three Known Sides):

When all three sides (a, b, c) are known, first compute the semi-perimeter (s):

s (Semi-Perimeter) = (a + b + c) ÷ 2
Area = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)]

Angles are then extracted using the Law of Cosines:

cos(C) = (a² + b² − c²) ÷ (2ab)   ⟹   ∠C = arccos[(a² + b² − c²) ÷ (2ab)]

3. SAS: Law of Cosines (Two Sides & Included Angle γ):

c = √(a² + b² − 2ab · cos γ),   Area = ½ × a × b × sin γ

Worked Example: Roof Pitch & Rafter Length

Suppose you are framing a gable roof with a span of 24 feet (run b = 12 ft from ridge to exterior wall) and a 6/12 pitch (meaning a rise of 6 inches per 12 inches of run, so total rise a = 6 ft):

  • Rise (a): 6.0 ft.
  • Run (b): 12.0 ft.
  • Rafter Length (c): √(6² + 12²) = √(36 + 144) = √180 ≈ 13.42 feet (13 ft 5 in).
  • Roof Pitch Angle: arctan(6/12) = arctan(0.5) ≈ 26.57°.
  • Gable End Triangular Area: 0.5 × 24 ft × 6 ft = 72.0 sq ft.

The Triangle Inequality Theorem

For any valid triangle with sides a, b, c, the sum of the lengths of any two sides must always be strictly greater than the length of the remaining side:

a + b > c,   a + c > b,   b + c > a

If you enter sides of 5, 8, and 15, the calculator flags the entry as an invalid triangle because 5 + 8 = 13 < 15. The sides cannot physically close.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Violating the Triangle Inequality: Entering three sides where the longest exceeds the sum of the other two.
  • Radian vs Degree Mode Confusion: Standard geometry uses degrees ($0^\circ$ to $180^\circ$); ensuring angles sum to exactly $180^\circ$.
  • The SSA Ambiguous Case: Specifying two sides and a non-included angle can yield zero, one, or two possible valid triangles.

Solving Right Triangle Geometry with a Comprehensive Triangle Calculator

A triangle calculator solves missing sides, interior angles, perimeter, and area for any right or oblique triangle. By inputting known sides and angles, it applies geometric principles and trigonometric functions (sin, cos, tan) to resolve the triangle instantly.

The Pythagorean Theorem for Right Triangles ($a^2 + b^2 = c^2$)

For any right triangle with perpendicular legs $a$ and $b$, the hypotenuse $c$ is calculated as $c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$. If the hypotenuse and one leg are known, the missing leg length is found using $a = \sqrt{c^2 - b^2}$.

Calculating Area Using Base $\times$ Height and Heron's Formula (SSS)

While standard right triangles use $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$, oblique triangles with three known sides use Heron's formula: $\text{Area} = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}$, where $s = (a + b + c) / 2$ is the semi-perimeter.

Solving Oblique Triangles with the Law of Sines and Law of Cosines

When triangles lack a 90-degree right angle, the Law of Sines ($\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}$) and Law of Cosines ($c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab \cos C$) solve configurations based on Side-Side-Side (SSS), Side-Angle-Side (SAS), or Angle-Side-Angle (ASA).

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Pythagorean theorem solve a right triangle?

For any right triangle with perpendicular legs $a$ and $b$, the hypotenuse $c$ is calculated as $c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$. If the hypotenuse and one leg are known, the missing leg is $a = \sqrt{c^2 - b^2}$.

How do you calculate the area of any triangle when only the three sides are known?

Use Heron's formula: first calculate the semi-perimeter $s = (a + b + c) / 2$, then the area equals $\text{Area} = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}$. No height measurement is needed.

What is the sum of angles in any planar triangle?

The interior angles of any planar triangle on a Euclidean surface always sum to exactly 180 degrees ($\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 180^\circ$). If two angles are known, the third is $180^\circ - (\alpha + \beta)$.

What is the 3-4-5 triangle rule in construction and layout?

A triangle with side lengths 3, 4, and 5 forms a true right-angle triangle ($3^2 + 4^2 = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5^2$). Builders use this ratio to square corners accurately when framing foundations, decks, and walls.
