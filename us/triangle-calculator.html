Pythagorean Theorem, Law of Cosines & Heron's Formula

For right triangles, side lengths follow a² + b² = c², with trigonometric ratios sin(θ) = opp/hyp, cos(θ) = adj/hyp, and tan(θ) = opp/adj. For oblique triangles with three known sides a, b, c, Heron's formula computes area as: Area = √(s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)), where s = (a+b+c)/2 is the semi-perimeter. Angles are solved using the Law of Cosines: cos(C) = (a² + b² - c²)/(2ab).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Pythagorean Theorem formula for right triangles?

The Pythagorean Theorem is a² + b² = c², where a and b are the lengths of the two perpendicular legs, and c is the length of the hypotenuse opposite the 90-degree right angle.

How do you calculate triangle area when all three sides are known (SSS)?

Use Heron's Formula: first compute semi-perimeter s = (a + b + c) / 2, then Area = √(s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)).

What do the interior angles of any triangle always add up to?

The sum of the three interior angles in any Euclidean planar triangle is always exactly 180 degrees (π radians).
