How this calculator works

Uses 2026 federal income-tax brackets (with standard deduction of $15,750 single / $31,500 married, indexed estimates), FICA at 6.2% Social Security (up to the wage-base cap of $184,500) + 1.45% Medicare (+0.9% additional Medicare tax over $200,000/$250,000), then 401(k) pre-tax and health insurance post- or pre-tax depending on plan. State and local income tax are not included — add your state's rate manually if applicable.

Verify current-year IRS numbers on IRS.gov. IRS releases inflation adjustments each autumn.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter your annual gross salary — the total pay before deductions, before bonuses.
  2. Pick your pay frequency (26 biweekly / 24 semi-monthly / 12 monthly / 52 weekly).
  3. Choose your filing status (Single or Married Filing Jointly).
  4. Add your 401(k) % and health-insurance premium per paycheck if known.
  5. Click Calculate take-home pay to see net pay per check plus a full deduction table.

The method behind the math

Federal income tax. Subtract traditional 401(k) contributions (pre-tax), then the standard deduction ($15,750 single / $31,500 MFJ for 2026 estimates), then run the remainder through the 2026 brackets — 10% up to $11,925, 12% to $48,475, 22% to $103,350, 24% to $197,300 for Single (MFJ thresholds roughly double).

FICA payroll taxes. Social Security: 6.2% of wages up to the 2026 wage-base cap of $184,500. Medicare: 1.45% with no cap, plus an Additional Medicare Tax of 0.9% on wages exceeding $200,000 single / $250,000 MFJ.

Then: subtract the health-insurance premium per paycheck, and divide the annual net by your pay frequency to get per-check take-home.

Worked example — $85,000 salary, biweekly

Noah earns $85,000, contributes 6% to his 401(k) ($5,100 pre-tax), and pays $120 per check for health premiums. Taxable wages after the 401(k) and the standard deduction land near $64,150. Federal income tax is roughly $9,027/year, Social Security $4,954, Medicare $1,159. His annual net after all deductions is $61,641, or $2,371 per biweekly paycheck.

Local rules and context

  • Payroll withholding uses Form W-4 elections; this calculator assumes the default (no extra withholding, no itemizing).
  • State income tax is NOT included — states range from 0% (TX, FL, WA, NV, SD, WY, AK, TN, NH) to over 13% (CA). Add your state's rate manually if applicable.
  • Social Security has a wage base cap; wages above it stop accruing SS tax but continue Medicare.
  • Roth 401(k) contributions are post-tax and would not reduce taxable wages — this calculator assumes traditional only.
  • Bonuses are typically withheld at a flat 22% supplemental federal rate — calculate them separately.

Practical tips

  • Update W-4 elections after any major life event (marriage, child, home purchase) so your withholding matches your liability.
  • Pre-tax deductions (401k, FSA, HSA) also reduce FICA — effectively 7.65% saved in addition to your marginal rate.
  • Review your paystub's YTD figures mid-year to catch any withholding drift before December.
  • If your income drops mid-year (layoff, unpaid leave), submit a new W-4 to avoid a big tax bill in April.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming gross × your tax bracket rate equals what's withheld — brackets are marginal, not flat.
  • Expecting Social Security to stop above the wage base if you have multiple employers — the cap applies per employer.
  • Comparing per-paycheck numbers across different pay frequencies (weekly checks are smaller than biweekly by design).
  • Forgetting employer-paid benefits (e.g. company-paid life insurance over $50,000) are taxable wages.
  • Ignoring state disability-family-leave withholding (CA, NJ, NY, RI, HI, WA).

Frequently asked questions

Is my state income tax included?

No. Each state sets its own rate (or has none at all — TX, FL, WA, etc.). Add your state's effective withholding rate manually or use your state's official calculator for precision.

Are 401(k) contributions subtracted before tax?

Yes, for traditional 401(k). Roth 401(k) is post-tax and would not reduce today's taxable wage.

How accurate is this paycheck calculator?

Within about 2 percent for federal tax and FICA on a simple W-4 with no extra withholding. Accuracy drops with nonstandard W-4 elections, itemized deductions, or noncash benefits.

Why is my first paycheck lower than later ones?

Usually proration — you worked fewer hours that period, or benefits start the following month. Health premiums are sometimes charged twice in a month with three biweekly pay periods.

What is the Social Security wage base for 2026?

Wages stop accruing the 6.2 percent Social Security portion above the annual wage base, but continue to accrue the 1.45 percent Medicare portion (and the 0.9 percent Additional Medicare tax above 200,000 dollars single or 250,000 dollars married).

What is the Additional Medicare Tax?

0.9 percent on employee wages above 200,000 dollars single or 250,000 dollars married filing jointly, on top of the normal 1.45 percent Medicare. Employers withhold it automatically once wages cross the threshold.

Do I get taxes back at year-end if I overpaid?

Yes. Overwithheld amounts are refunded after filing your federal return, typically within 21 days of the IRS accepting an e-filed return.

Is bonus income taxed differently?

Supplemental wages can be withheld at a flat 22 percent federal rate (37 percent above 1 million dollars), but actual tax at filing is based on your bracket. Overwithheld bonus tax is refunded at filing.

Do Roth 401(k) contributions reduce my paycheck now?

They reduce take-home pay more than traditional 401(k) because they are post-tax, but produce tax-free qualified withdrawals at retirement. This calculator models traditional 401(k) only.

Are FSA or HSA contributions pre-tax?

Yes. Payroll-deducted FSA and HSA contributions reduce taxable wages for both FICA and federal income tax, effectively saving the 7.65 percent FICA share plus your marginal rate.
