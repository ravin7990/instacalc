The 401(k) Calculator estimates how your retirement balance grows when contributions, employer matching, and compound returns work together — the single most powerful force in American retirement savings.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter your current age and the age you plan to retire (67 is the full Social Security retirement age).
  2. Enter your current 401(k) balance.
  3. Enter your annual salary and your contribution percentage (the 2026 limit is $24,500 employee).
  4. Add your employer match (a typical formula is 50% of contributions up to 6% of salary — "free money").
  5. Set an expected annual return (7% is a reasonable inflation-adjusted estimate for a diversified stock portfolio).
  6. Click Calculate — you'll see the ending balance, total contributions, employer match earned, and total growth.

The method behind the math

Growth compounds annually: Balance(t+1) = (Balance(t) + contributions + match) × (1 + return). Employer match earned = your salary × match-percent × match-limit (e.g. 50% × 6% = 3% of salary free each year if you contribute at least 6%). Compound growth over N years uses the standard future-value formula summing each year's contribution and match with the return factor.

Worked example

At age 30 with $25,000 saved, contributing 6% of an $85,000 salary ($5,100/yr) with a 50%-to-6% employer match ($2,550/yr free money), growing at 7%: by age 67 the balance hits roughly $1.1 million — of which only about $215,000 came from your paycheck. The match and compound growth produced the rest (~$885,000).

Rules and regulations that affect your result

  • 2026 employee contribution limit: $24,500 ($30,500 if age 50+ with catch-up).
  • 2026 total annual additions cap (employee + employer + profit-sharing): $70,000.
  • Employee contributions are pre-tax (traditional) unless you choose Roth 401(k), which is post-tax but grows tax-free.
  • Employer matches are ALWAYS pre-tax and immediately vested if the plan has immediate vesting — check your Summary Plan Description.
  • Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) begin at age 73 for traditional 401(k) — not Roth 401(k) since SECURE 2.0.
  • Early withdrawal before 59½ usually triggers a 10% penalty plus ordinary income tax.

Practical tips

  • Always contribute at least to the full employer match — it's a guaranteed 50-100% instant return.
  • Increase your contribution rate by 1% each year (many plans offer auto-escalation) — the incremental cost is tiny, the retirement impact is huge.
  • Keep your 401(k) when leaving a job (roll to IRA or new employer) rather than cashing out — cashing a $50,000 balance at 35 could cost you $500,000+ at retirement.
  • Low-cost index funds (expense ratio <0.20%) outperform the average active fund over 20 years.
  • Use "in服务" if available — the auto-increase feature.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Cashing out when changing jobs — taxes plus the 10% penalty plus lost compounding.
  • Contributing less than the full match — leaving guaranteed free money behind.
  • Picking the default (often a money-market) fund rather than the target-date or index fund.
  • Ignoring fees — a 1% annual fee over 30 years can eat ~25% of a portfolio.
  • Stopping contributions during recessions — you lock in losses and miss the recovery.

The Power of Compound Growth in Your 401(k) Plan

A 401k calculator illustrates how pre-tax salary deferrals transform into a substantial retirement nest egg over decades. Because contributions are invested before federal and state income taxes are withheld, your full dollar amount compounds, reinvesting capital gains and dividends tax-deferred until distribution.

Maximizing Your 401k Employer Match Calculator Advantage

Many employers match employee contributions up to a specific percentage, such as dollar-for-dollar on the first 3% or 50 cents per dollar up to 6%. Failing to contribute enough to claim the full employer match means leaving guaranteed, risk-free compensation on the table.

2026 401(k) Contribution Limits and Catch-Up Provisions

For 2026, the IRS employee elective deferral cap is $23,500 per year. Workers aged 50 and older can take advantage of an additional $7,500 catch-up contribution ($31,000 total). Maxing out these tax-advantaged accounts significantly reduces current taxable income while accelerating retirement wealth.

401(k) Retirement Withdrawal Rules and Early Penalty Penalties

Qualified withdrawals begin without penalty at age 59½ and are taxed as ordinary income. Withdrawing funds prior to 59½ incurs ordinary taxes plus a 10% IRS early distribution penalty, unless qualifying under specific exemptions like the Rule of 55 or medical hardship.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does an employer 401(k) match work and why is it free money?

Employers often match contributions up to a percentage of salary—such as 50% on the first 6% you contribute. Contributing at least enough to capture the full match yields an instant, risk-free 50% to 100% return on your investment dollars.

What is the 401(k) contribution limit for 2026?

For 2026, the standard employee elective deferral limit is $23,500. Workers aged 50 and older can contribute an additional $7,500 catch-up contribution, bringing their total tax-advantaged annual contribution ceiling to $31,000.

What penalties apply if I withdraw from a 401(k) before age 59½?

Non-qualified early withdrawals incur ordinary federal and state income taxes plus a 10% IRS early distribution penalty. Exceptions include qualified medical expenses, permanent disability, birth/adoption expenses, or using the Rule of 55 upon separation from service.

How much can a 401(k) grow over 30 years with consistent savings?

Contributing $500 monthly with a 4% company match at an average 8% annual return accumulates over $850,000 in 30 years. Thanks to tax-deferred compound interest, over 70% of the final balance comes from reinvested investment gains.
