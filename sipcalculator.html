How the SIP Calculator Projects Mutual Fund Returns

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) allows investors to allocate a fixed sum into mutual fund schemes every month. Our SIP calculator computes future wealth using the monthly compounding formula: FV = P * [((1 + r)^n - 1) / r] * (1 + r). By automating consistent monthly investing, investors leverage rupee cost averaging to buy more fund units during market pullbacks and fewer units during rallies.

Step-by-Step SIP Return Calculation with Real Indian Examples

Consider a monthly SIP contribution of ₹10,000 in an equity mutual fund delivering an assumed 12% annualized return over 15 years. Your total out-of-pocket investment amounts to ₹18,00,000 (₹18 Lakh). Thanks to compounding growth, your estimated capital gain is ₹32,45,760, yielding a total maturity corpus of ₹50,45,760 (~₹50.5 Lakh). Compounding accelerates exponentially in later years.

Equity Mutual Fund Taxation: LTCG 12.5% & STCG 20% in India

Under the Finance Act 2024, equity mutual fund taxation in India follows updated thresholds. Long-term capital gains (LTCG) on equity investments held for longer than 12 months are taxed at 12.5% on gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year. Short-term capital gains (STCG) on units redeemed within 12 months are taxed at a flat rate of 20%, without indexation benefits.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does a SIP calculator calculate mutual fund returns?

A SIP calculator calculates future returns using compound interest applied to monthly instalments: FV = P * [((1 + r)^n - 1) / r] * (1 + r). Here P is the monthly deposit, r is the monthly rate of return, and n is the total number of instalments.

What is an ideal expected rate of return for equity SIP in India?

Historically, diversified Indian equity mutual funds (such as Nifty 50 or broad multicap funds) have delivered long-term average annualized returns between 12% and 15%. However, returns fluctuate with market cycles and are not legally guaranteed by fund houses or SEBI.

How are mutual fund SIP gains taxed in India under Finance Act 2024?

Long-term capital gains (LTCG) on equity mutual funds held for over 12 months are taxed at 12.5% for gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year. Short-term capital gains (STCG) on units held for 12 months or less are taxed at a flat 20%.

Is a SIP better than a lumpsum investment for salaried individuals?

For salaried individuals with recurring monthly cash flow, a SIP is typically superior because it enforces financial discipline and leverages rupee-cost averaging. This mitigates market timing risk by purchasing more mutual fund units during market corrections and fewer during market rallies.
