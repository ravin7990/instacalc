How to Calculate Concrete Materials (Cement, Sand & Aggregate)

The 1.54 Dry Volume Factor

When dry materials (cement powder, fine sand, coarse aggregate) are mixed with water, the fine particles fill void spaces between aggregate rocks. Therefore, dry volume decreases by approximately 54%. To prepare 1 m³ of wet compacted concrete, civil engineers multiply by 1.54 to determine dry batch quantities.

Standard Concrete Grade Mix Ratios

  • M10 (1:3:6): Characteristic strength 10 MPa. Ideal for leveling beds and mass foundations.
  • M15 (1:2:4): Characteristic strength 15 MPa. Used for non-structural pathways and plain concrete.
  • M20 (1:1.5:3): Characteristic strength 20 MPa. The universal benchmark for residential RCC slabs, beams, and columns.
  • M25 (1:1:2): Characteristic strength 25 MPa. High-strength mix for heavy load-bearing structural members.
How many bags of cement are needed for 1 cubic meter of M20 concrete?

For standard M20 grade concrete (1:1.5:3 ratio), you will need approximately 8.2 to 8.4 bags of 50 kg cement per cubic meter of wet concrete, including standard 5% site wastage.

How much sand and aggregate is required per cubic meter?

For 1 m³ of M20 mix, you will need approximately 0.44 m³ (15.5 CFT) of clean fine sand and 0.88 m³ (31.1 CFT) of coarse aggregate (20mm gravel).

How is rebar steel weight estimated for RCC elements?

Steel reinforcement is standardly estimated by structural volume: Slabs require ~80 kg/m³, Beams ~120 kg/m³, Columns ~145 kg/m³, and Footings ~80 kg/m³.
