How the Gratuity Calculator Applies the Payment of Gratuity Act 1972

Gratuity is a statutory monetary benefit provided by employers to employees in recognition of continuous, dedicated service. Our gratuity calculator accurately computes your gratuity entitlement upon resignation, retirement, or superannuation based on your last drawn salary and total completed years of service under the Payment of Gratuity Act 1972.

The 15/26 Formula & Last Drawn Salary Computation

For employees covered under the Act, gratuity is computed using the legal formula: Gratuity = (15 * Last Drawn Basic Salary + DA * Completed Years of Service) / 26. Here, 26 represents the total working days in a calendar month, and 15 represents half a month's wages (15 days) per year of completed service.

Tax Exemption Thresholds: The ₹20 Lakh Limit under Section 10(10)

Under Section 10(10) of the Income Tax Act, the maximum lifetime tax-exempt gratuity for non-government private sector employees covered under the Act is ₹20,00,000 (₹20 Lakh). Any gratuity received in excess of ₹20 Lakh is added to taxable income and taxed at marginal slab rates. For government employees, gratuity is 100% exempt from income tax.

Service Eligibility, Resignation, Retirement & Forfeiture Rules

To qualify for gratuity, an employee must complete a minimum of 5 years of continuous service with the same organization. The 5-year tenure condition is waived if employment terminates due to death or permanent disablement. Furthermore, service exceeding 6 months in your final year (e.g., 6 years 7 months) is legally rounded up to the next full year (7 years).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the formula for calculating gratuity under the Payment of Gratuity Act 1972?

For employees covered under the Act, gratuity is calculated as: Gratuity = (15 * Last Drawn Basic Salary + DA * Completed Years of Service) / 26. Here 26 represents the working days in a month, and 15 represents 15 days of wages per year of service.

What is the minimum service requirement to become eligible for gratuity in India?

Under Section 4 of the Payment of Gratuity Act 1972, an employee is eligible for gratuity upon completing a minimum of 5 continuous years of service with the same employer. The 5-year requirement is waived in cases of death or permanent disablement.

What is the maximum tax-free gratuity exemption limit in India?

Under Section 10(10) of the Income Tax Act, the maximum lifetime tax-exempt gratuity for private sector employees covered under the Act is ₹20 Lakh. For central and state government employees, the full gratuity amount received is completely exempt from income tax.

How are fractions of service years treated in gratuity calculations?

If an employee has worked for more than 6 months in their final year of service (e.g., 7 years and 7 months), it is rounded up to the next full year (8 years). If the fraction is 6 months or less (e.g., 7 years and 4 months), it is rounded down to 7 years.
