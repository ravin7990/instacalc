How the EPF Calculator Models Employer & Employee Contributions

The Employee Provident Fund (EPF) is a mandatory retirement savings scheme managed by the Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) for salaried professionals in establishments with 20 or more employees. Our EPF calculator models monthly employee contributions, employer contributions, annual wage increments, and compounding interest until retirement at age 58.

The 12% Wage Split: EPF Accumulation vs EPS Pension Diversion

The employee contributes 12% of Basic Salary + Dearness Allowance (DA) directly into their EPF account. The employer also contributes 12%, but this is split: 8.33% goes toward the Employee Pension Scheme (EPS, capped at ₹1,250 per month based on the ₹15,000 statutory wage ceiling), and the remaining 3.67% enters the employee's EPF account.

Section 10(11) Tax Rules on EPF Contributions Exceeding ₹2.5 Lakh

Under Section 10(11) of the Income Tax Act (introduced in Finance Act 2021), if an employee's total annual contribution to EPF and Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF) exceeds ₹2,50,000 (₹2.5 Lakh), interest earned on the excess contribution is taxable annually as income at the employee's marginal tax slab rate, tracked via a separate non-taxable vs taxable contribution ledger.

Boosting Retirement Corpus with Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF)

Salaried employees can voluntarily increase their retirement savings by contributing up to 100% of their Basic Salary and DA into the Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF). VPF contributions earn the identical high interest rate declared by the EPFO (currently notified at 8.25% per annum) and share the same sovereign safety and tax exemption features as standard EPF.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are employee and employer EPF contributions split in India?

The employee contributes 12% of Basic Salary + Dearness Allowance (DA) directly to the EPF. The employer matches 12%, but splits it: 8.33% goes to the Employee Pension Scheme (EPS, capped at ₹15,000 wage ceiling = ₹1,250/mo) and the remaining 3.67% enters the employee's EPF account.

What is the current EPFO interest rate and how is it credited?

The Central Board of Trustees (EPFO) recommends the annual interest rate (notified at 8.25% for recent financial years). Interest compounds annually but is calculated on monthly running balances and officially credited to the employee passbook at the close of the financial year.

Is EPF interest taxable above the ₹2.5 Lakh annual threshold?

Yes. As per the Finance Act 2021, if an employee's total annual contribution to EPF and VPF exceeds ₹2.5 Lakh (or ₹5 Lakh where no employer contribution exists), interest earned on the excess contribution is taxable annually as income at your applicable slab rate.

Can I increase my contribution through Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF)?

Yes. An employee can voluntarily contribute up to 100% of their Basic Pay and DA into the Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF). VPF funds earn the same high EPFO interest rate and follow identical lock-in and withdrawal rules as standard EPF.
