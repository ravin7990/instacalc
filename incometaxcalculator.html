How this calculator works

This calculator uses the new-regime slab rates and the old-regime slab rates with Section 87A rebate and 4% Health & Education Cess for residents under 60.

New-regime slabs (FY 2026-27)

  • ₹0 – ₹4,00,000: Nil
  • ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000: 5%
  • ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000: 10%
  • ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000: 15%
  • ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000: 20%
  • ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000: 25%
  • Above ₹24,00,000: 30%
  • Standard deduction (salaried): ₹75,000
  • Section 87A rebate: up to ₹60,000 for income up to ₹12,00,000 (₹12.75 L salaried after standard deduction)

Old-regime slabs (FY 2026-27)

  • ₹0 – ₹2,50,000: Nil
  • ₹2,50,001 – ₹5,00,000: 5%
  • ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000: 20%
  • Above ₹10,00,000: 30%
  • Section 87A rebate: up to ₹12,500 for income up to ₹5,00,000

Verify the current slabs at the Income Tax Department e-filing portal — slabs change with each Budget.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter your gross annual salary — the CTC figure before deductions.
  2. Enter your old-regime deductions — 80C + 80D + HRA + home-loan interest + other 80-series total.
  3. Add NPS 80CCD(1B) separately (up to ₹50,000, old-regime only).
  4. Click Compare both regimes — both tax figures appear and the winner is declared.

The method behind the comparison

New regime: subtract the ₹75,000 standard deduction for salaried taxpayers, apply the FY 2026-27 slabs (5% from ₹4-8 L, 10% from ₹8-12 L, 15% from ₹12-16 L, 20% from ₹16-20 L, 25% from ₹20-24 L, 30% above ₹24 L), apply the ₹60,000 Section 87A rebate when taxable income is ₹12 L or less, then add 4% Health & Education Cess.

Old regime: subtract your entered deductions + NPS, apply the legacy slabs (5% from ₹2.5-5 L, 20% from ₹5-10 L, 30% above ₹10 L), apply the ₹12,500 Section 87A rebate at ₹5 L or below, then add cess.

Worked example — ₹12 lakh salary

Priya earns ₹12,00,000 gross. Under the new regime, the ₹75,000 standard deduction brings taxable income to ₹11,25,000, which is under the ₹12 L cap — the Section 87A rebate of ₹60,000 wipes the tax down to almost nothing (about ₹15,000 including cess, effectively near-zero).

Under the old regime with ₹2,50,000 deductions plus ₹50,000 NPS, taxable income drops to ₹9,00,000, producing ₹96,200 in tax after rebate and cess. New regime wins by about ₹96,200.

Local rules that affect your result

  • Section 87A new-regime rebate caps at ₹60,000 and phases out above ₹12 L taxable income. A marginal relief clause limits the tax jump when crossing ₹12 L.
  • Standard deduction under the old regime is much lower — ₹50,000 for salaried taxpayers.
  • Surcharge kicks in above ₹50 L income (10% from ₹50 L-₹1 Cr, 15% from ₹1-2 Cr, 25% above ₹2 Cr).
  • The new regime allows almost no deductions beyond the standard deduction and employer NPS contribution.
  • The old regime still wins with heavy existing home loans or substantial 80C + 80D commitments.

Practical tips

  • Run this comparison every year — slabs and rebate thresholds change with each Budget.
  • Collect rent receipts and the landlord's PAN for HRA (rent over ₹1 L per year).
  • Salaried taxpayers without business income can switch regimes annually.
  • You have until March 31 to invest for the relevant F.Y. — don't wait for the last week.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting that NPS 80CCD(1B) is capped at ₹50,000 and counts separately from the ₹1.5 L 80C cap.
  • Double-counting the same deduction under both regimes in the comparison.
  • Ignoring employer NPS contributions — they count as a new-regime deduction too.
  • Applying surcharge manually when the calculator doesn't include it (relevant at ₹50 L+).
  • Confusing AY 2027-28 (assessment year) with FY 2026-27 (financial year).

Frequently asked questions

Which tax regime should I pick for FY 2026-27?

Compare both regimes with your actual deductions in this calculator. Generally the new regime wins below roughly ₹4-5 lakh of deductions; the old regime can win with heavy HRA, home-loan interest, 80C, and NPS.

Does HRA exemption apply in the new tax regime?

No. HRA, 80C, 80D, LTA, and most Section 80 deductions are old-regime only. The new regime allows only the standard deduction and employer NPS contribution.

Is surcharge included in this calculator?

No. Surcharge applies above ₹50 lakh income (10% over ₹50 lakh, 25% over ₹1 crore). Add it manually if your income crosses those thresholds.

What is the standard deduction under the new tax regime for FY 2026-27?

₹75,000 for salaried taxpayers, applied before slab rates. The old regime keeps the ₹50,000 standard deduction. Pensioners get ₹75,000 too under the new regime since FY 2025-26.

What is the Section 87A rebate in the new regime?

Up to ₹60,000 rebate when total taxable income is ₹12 lakh or less, making tax effectively zero up to about ₹12.75 lakh for salaried taxpayers after the standard deduction. Above ₹12 lakh the rebate phases out and only marginal relief applies.

Can I switch between old and new regimes every year?

Salaried taxpayers without business income can switch every year by informing their employer or choosing the regime while filing. Taxpayers with business or professional income can switch back to the old regime only once in a lifetime.

Does the new regime allow home-loan interest deduction?

Generally no for self-occupied property. An exception exists for a let-out property under Section 24(b), but not the ₹2 lakh self-occupied deduction the old regime allows.

What documents do I need to claim 80C under the old regime?

Proof of each investment: LIC premium receipts, PPF passbook updates, ELSS or NSC statements, children's tuition receipts, home-loan principal certificate from the bank, EPF contribution statements, and health-insurance premium receipts for 80D.

Is corporate tax or capital gains covered by this calculator?

No. It covers resident individual salary and deductions. Short-term and long-term capital gains have separate rates and should be calculated separately.

What is the health and education cess rate?

4 percent on total income tax plus surcharge. It applies equally under both regimes and is always added last.
