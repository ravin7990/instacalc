How the Income Tax Calculator Computes Tax under FY 2025-26 Slabs

Our income tax calculator models personal tax liabilities under the Indian Income Tax Act for Assessment Year 2026-27 (Financial Year 2025-26). By entering your gross annual salary, rental income, capital gains, and eligible exemptions, you can compare tax liabilities under the default New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC) versus the Old Tax Regime to make an informed regime choice.

The New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC): Default Slabs & ₹75,000 Deduction

Under Section 115BAC for salaried individuals in FY 2025-26: Nil tax up to ₹3 Lakh; 5% from ₹3 Lakh to ₹7 Lakh; 10% from ₹7 Lakh to ₹10 Lakh; 15% from ₹10 Lakh to ₹12 Lakh; 20% from ₹12 Lakh to ₹15 Lakh; and 30% above ₹15 Lakh. Salaried taxpayers also receive an enhanced ₹75,000 standard deduction (up from ₹50,000) and family pension deduction up to ₹25,000.

Section 87A Rebate: Why Salaried Incomes up to ₹7.75 Lakh Pay Zero Tax

Salaried taxpayers earning up to ₹7,75,000 pay zero income tax under the New Tax Regime. The ₹75,000 standard deduction automatically reduces taxable salary to ₹7,00,000. Under Section 87A, any taxable income up to ₹7,00,000 qualifies for a full 100% tax rebate (up to ₹25,000), completely eliminating tax liability before cess.

Old vs New Regime Decision Matrix: Choosing the Right Option

Choosing between regimes depends on your total eligible itemized deductions. The New Regime offers lower tax rates without requiring locked savings. The Old Regime remains advantageous only if your combined deductions—including Section 80C (₹1.5L), 80D health insurance (₹25k-₹50k), HRA exemptions, and Section 24(b) home loan interest (up to ₹2L)—exceed approximately ₹3.75 Lakh to ₹4.0 Lakh.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the New Tax Regime slabs for salaried individuals in FY 2025-26?

Under Section 115BAC: Nil up to ₹3,00,000; 5% from ₹3,00,001 to ₹7,00,000; 10% from ₹7,00,001 to ₹10,00,000; 15% from ₹10,00,001 to ₹12,00,000; 20% from ₹12,00,001 to ₹15,00,000; and 30% above ₹15,00,000. Salaried individuals also receive a ₹75,000 standard deduction.

Why is income up to ₹7.75 Lakh completely tax-free under the New Regime for salaried employees?

A salaried person receives an automatic ₹75,000 standard deduction, reducing gross salary of ₹7.75 Lakh to taxable income of ₹7.00 Lakh. Taxable income up to ₹7.00 Lakh qualifies for a full 100% tax rebate under Section 87A, resulting in zero net tax payable.

Which tax regime is more advantageous: Old Regime or New Regime?

For most individuals, the New Tax Regime is more advantageous due to lower slab rates and the ₹75,000 standard deduction without locking money in investments. The Old Regime is beneficial only if your total itemized deductions (80C, 80D, HRA, home loan interest) exceed roughly ₹3.75 Lakh to ₹4.0 Lakh.

What surcharge and cess are applicable on Indian income tax liabilities?

A mandatory 4% Health and Education Cess is levied on the total calculated income tax and surcharge. Surcharge rates under the New Tax Regime apply to high earners: 10% for income between ₹50 Lakh and ₹1 Crore, 15% for ₹1 Crore to ₹2 Crore, and a capped 25% above ₹2 Crore.
