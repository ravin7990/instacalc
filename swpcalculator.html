How to Use this Calculator

  1. Enter the invested amount.
  2. Enter the monthly withdrawal.
  3. Enter the expected annual return.
  4. Calculate — the withdrawal-duration analysis appears.

Formula and Method

Method: each month the remaining balance earns annual-return ÷ 12, then the withdrawal is deducted: balance = (balance × (1 + r)) − withdrawal.

Worked Example

₹20 lakh invested, ₹10,000 monthly withdrawal, 12% p.a. — first-month interest = ₹20 lakh × 1% = ₹20,000; balance = ₹20 lakh + 20,000 − 10,000 = ₹21.10 lakh.

Rules and Regulations

  • SWP exit loads apply per redemption, typically within the first year.
  • Withdrawals begin one month after setup.
  • Each withdrawal's gains attract capital-gains tax (not included here).

Use Cases tips

Retirement income, education funding, and long-term passive income — a 4–5% annual withdrawal rate is broadly considered sustainable.

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming a high withdrawal rate (6–7% annual) — down years deplete the corpus fast.
  • Ignoring tax — SWP gains are taxable.

How the SWP Calculator Computes Monthly Retirement Income

A Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) allows mutual fund investors to redeem a specified rupee amount from their accumulated mutual fund corpus at regular intervals (typically monthly). While you draw regular income, the remaining balance stays invested in the market, continuing to generate compound returns and offsetting the impact of ongoing withdrawals.

Tax Superiority: SWP Payouts vs Bank Fixed Deposit Monthly Interest

Bank fixed deposit interest is fully taxable as income at your marginal income tax slab rate (up to 30% plus cess). In contrast, an SWP redemption is treated as a sale of mutual fund units. Each payout consists largely of your own tax-free principal capital, with only the capital gains portion subject to tax (equity LTCG at 12.5% above ₹1.25 Lakh), substantially maximizing your post-tax monthly cash flow.

Sustainable Withdrawal Rates: Preserving Capital Against Inflation

To prevent depleting your retirement nest egg prematurely, financial planners recommend a conservative annual withdrawal rate of 6% to 8% for balanced advantage or hybrid mutual funds yielding 10% to 12% annualized returns. Keeping withdrawal rates below fund growth preserves the real purchasing power of your capital over a 20 to 30-year retirement lifespan.

Setting Up an SWP in Equity vs Hybrid Debt Mutual Funds

For near-term monthly cash flows (the next 1 to 3 years), setting up an SWP from conservative hybrid, equity savings, or short-duration debt funds provides stability and shields monthly payouts from severe equity market downturns. The remaining long-term corpus can compound in aggressive equity funds to fund future withdrawal increments.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) in mutual funds?

A Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) is a facility that allows mutual fund investors to redeem a predetermined fixed amount from their accumulated corpus at specified intervals (typically monthly). The remaining capital stays invested, continuing to generate compound returns.

Why is an SWP more tax-efficient than a bank Fixed Deposit for monthly income?

Bank FD interest is taxed at your applicable income tax slab rate (up to 30% plus surcharge). In contrast, each SWP payout consists of a principal component (tax-free) and a capital gain component, where equity LTCG is taxed at only 12.5% above ₹1.25 Lakh.

What is a safe withdrawal rate for an SWP to avoid exhausting my corpus?

A withdrawal rate of 6% to 8% per annum is widely considered sustainable for balanced or hybrid mutual funds delivering 10% to 12% annualized returns. Keeping withdrawal rates below fund growth ensures your principal remains intact or continues expanding over multi-decade retirements.

Can I increase or decrease my monthly SWP payout amount later?

Yes. Investors have complete liquidity and flexibility to modify withdrawal amounts, change payment dates, pause withdrawals, or redeem their entire remaining balance at any time without punitive surrender charges once the scheme exit load period expires.
