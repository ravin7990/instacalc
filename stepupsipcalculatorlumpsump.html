How to use: three steps

Set your Current Monthly SIP, then the Annual Step-up you plan (e.g. 10%), the Expected Return, and the Tenure. The table updates instantly and the CSV button downloads the full projection. Step-up amount = current SIP multiplied by one plus the step-up rate, applied every January-equivalent anniversary.

Worked example

Example: a ₹10,000 SIP with a 10% yearly step-up for 15 years at 12% expected return invests about ₹37.9 lakh and can reach roughly ₹78 lakh — versus about ₹50 lakh from the same ₹10,000 held flat — because the yearly top-up rides compounding from year one. Run your own numbers above; the comparison chart shows the two curves separating in the middle years.

Practical tips and common mistakes

  • Common mistake: assuming the step-up % equals your salary hike. Net savings rate grows slower than CTC — model 60–70% of your increment as a realistic step-up.
  • Common mistake: stopping the top-up after one year. The compounding advantage shows up in years 8–12; quitting early forfeits most of the gain.
  • Tip: keep the first SIP affordable. A ₹20,000 SIP you stop in month 8 under-performs a ₹10,000 SIP plus consistent top-ups that run 15 years.
  • Tip: use the flat-SIP line on the chart as your minimum-trust benchmark — if you wouldn't sustain even that, renegotiate the step-up, not the base.

Rules and regulation notes

AMC top-up mandates require the stepped SIP to respect the scheme's minimum application amount; step-up intervals are commonly restricted to 6- or 12-month cycles, with the increment in multiples of ₹500/₹1,000. Top-up registrations can typically be cancelled prospectively without touching the base SIP.

How the Step-Up SIP calculation works

Each year, your monthly SIP amount is multiplied by (1 + step-up %). Within a year the SIP is invested monthly and grows at the monthly-equivalent of the expected annual return, compounding monthly — the same method used on our main SIP calculator. The flat-SIP comparison keeps the initial amount constant over the same tenure.

Step-up SIPs suit salaried professionals whose income rises each year: your savings rate scales with your salary instead of your purchasing power absorbing the raise. The extra corpus column shows exactly what that discipline is worth.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a step-up SIP?

A step-up (or top-up) SIP increases your monthly SIP amount by a fixed percentage every year, so your investment grows in line with your income instead of staying flat.

What is a good step-up percentage?

Most planners use 5–15% yearly. A common starting point matches your expected annual increment — 10% is a widely used conservative assumption.

How much more corpus does a 10% step-up create?

It depends on tenure and returns — over 15–20 years a 10% annual step-up commonly produces roughly 1.5× to 2× the corpus of a flat SIP on the same starting amount. Use the calculator above for your exact numbers.

Can I do a step-up SIP in any mutual fund?

Most AMCs and platforms (Groww, Zerodha Coin, Kuvera, MF Utility) let you register an annual top-up mandate at purchase time; otherwise you can manually start a new SIP each year.

What is the minimum top-up amount?

AMCs typically require the top-up to be in multiples of ₹500 or ₹1,000, differing per scheme. Check the scheme's step-up facility terms.

Does the step-up SIP calculator account for inflation?

The calculator shows nominal corpus. If you want a real (inflation-adjusted) target, use our main SIP calculator's inflation-adjustment mode with your own inflation assumption.

Is step-up SIP better than investing a flat higher amount from day one?

If you can afford a higher SIP today, investing more from the start wins because money compounds longer. Step-up is for when today's budget is limited but income is expected to grow.

Can I pause or stop the step-up?

Yes — registered top-up mandates can usually be cancelled or modified; the base SIP itself continues. Check your AMC's rules for notice periods.

What return rate should I assume for equity SIPs?

Historically diversified equity funds in India have delivered roughly 10–12% annualised over long periods, but returns are not guaranteed. Run the plan at a conservative rate first.
