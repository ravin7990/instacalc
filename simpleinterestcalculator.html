How the Simple Interest Calculator Computes Linear Loan Interest

Simple interest is an elementary method of calculating interest charges where interest is assessed strictly on the original principal sum for the entire duration of the loan. Our simple interest calculator quickly determines total interest payable, total repayment amount, and effective monthly cost for personal loans, short-term promissory notes, and informal lending.

The Standard SI Formula: SI = (P × R × T) / 100

Simple interest is calculated using the universal mathematical formula: SI = (P * R * T) / 100, where P is the principal amount borrowed or invested, R is the annual interest rate percentage, and T is the time duration in years. Total maturity repayment is calculated simply as: Amount = Principal + Simple Interest (A = P + SI).

Where Simple Interest is Used: Promissory Notes & Flat-Rate Loans

While commercial banks use compound reducing balance interest for retail mortgages and home loans, simple interest is still widely applied in short-term peer-to-peer promissory notes, agricultural seasonal credit, employee advances, statutory late payment tax penalties, and flat-rate commercial vehicle financing quotes.

Flat Interest vs Reducing Balance: Uncovering Hidden Loan Costs

Borrowers must be cautious of car loan quotes advertising seemingly low 'flat interest rates' (e.g., 7% flat). Because a flat loan charges simple interest on the original principal throughout the entire tenure without accounting for principal repaid monthly, a 7% flat rate actually equals an effective reducing balance APR of approximately 13% to 14%.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is simple interest and what is the mathematical formula?

Simple interest is a method of calculating interest where interest is accrued solely on the original principal amount throughout the entire loan tenure. The formula is: SI = (P * R * T) / 100, where P is principal, R is annual rate of interest, and T is time in years.

What is the primary difference between simple interest and compound interest?

In simple interest, interest is calculated only on the initial principal sum, resulting in a constant interest charge each period. In compound interest, accumulated interest is added back to the principal, causing interest to be earned on past interest.

Where is simple interest typically utilized in Indian financial markets?

Simple interest is commonly applied in short-term promissory notes, informal lending between individuals, certain agricultural loans, auto loan flat-rate quotations, and penalty fee calculations on delayed utility and statutory tax payments.

How do you calculate simple interest when the time period is given in months or days?

To calculate simple interest for months, divide months by 12 (T = months / 12). For days, divide days by 365 (or 366 in a leap year: T = days / 365). Plug this fractional value of T directly into SI = (P * R * T) / 100.
