How the CAGR Calculator Determines Geometric Annualized Returns

The Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) is the standardized metric used by investors and financial analysts to measure the smoothed annual return of an investment over multiple years. Our CAGR calculator determines the precise annualized growth rate for stocks, mutual fund portfolios, real estate properties, and business revenue metrics.

The Mathematical CAGR Formula: [(Ending / Beginning)^(1/n)] - 1

CAGR is computed using the geometric growth formula: CAGR = [(Ending Value / Beginning Value)^(1 / n)] - 1, where n is the total investment horizon in years. For example, if a property purchased for ₹25 Lakh in 2016 is sold for ₹60 Lakh in 2026 (10 years later), your investment achieved an annualized CAGR of 9.15%, outpacing general consumer inflation.

Why CAGR is Superior to Arithmetic Mean for Volatile Stock Portfolios

Arithmetic average returns produce misleading figures when evaluating volatile investments. For instance, if an equity portfolio surges +50% in year one and drops -50% in year two, the arithmetic average suggests an average annual return of 0%. However, your actual capital dropped from ₹100 to ₹150, then to ₹75—a real net loss of 25%. CAGR accurately captures this loss as a -13.4% annual rate.

CAGR vs Absolute Return vs XIRR: When to Use Each Metric

Understanding when to deploy each return metric ensures proper portfolio benchmarking: use Absolute Return for simple point-to-point trades under 12 months; use CAGR for multi-year lumpsum stock and real estate holdings; and use XIRR (Extended Internal Rate of Return) for mutual fund SIPs and portfolios with multiple staggered cash inflows and withdrawals.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is CAGR and how does the calculator compute it?

CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate) represents the constant annual rate of return required for an investment to grow from its initial value to its final balance over a specified number of years. The formula is: CAGR = [(Ending Value / Beginning Value)^(1 / n)] - 1.

Why is CAGR superior to average annual return for multi-year investments?

Average annual returns use arithmetic mean which distorts true performance due to compounding and volatility (e.g., +50% followed by -50% equals a net 25% loss, yet the arithmetic average appears to be 0%). CAGR uses geometric progression, reflecting your exact annualized capital growth.

What are the limitations of relying exclusively on CAGR for portfolio analysis?

CAGR smooths out all intermediate market volatility, creating the illusion of steady growth when an asset may have experienced dramatic swings. Additionally, CAGR evaluates only single initial and ending values, making it unsuitable for recurring investments like SIPs (which require XIRR).

What is a good CAGR for equity portfolios in the Indian stock market?

Historically, Indian benchmark indices (like Nifty 50 and Sensex) have delivered long-term 10-year and 15-year CAGRs of roughly 12% to 14%. A consistent portfolio CAGR of 12% to 15% outpaces consumer inflation (5-6%) and drives substantial real wealth expansion.
