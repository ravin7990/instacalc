How the PPF Calculator Models 15-Year Sovereign Compounding

The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a premier 15-year government-backed savings scheme offering guaranteed, tax-free returns. You can deposit between ₹500 and ₹1,50,000 per financial year. Our PPF calculator projects annual interest earnings, year-on-year balance accumulation, loan eligibility (from year 3 to 6), partial withdrawal limits (from year 7), and 15-year maturity proceeds.

The Crucial 5th of the Month Deposit Rule for Maximum Interest

Under PPF rules, interest is calculated on the lowest balance recorded between the close of the 5th day and the last day of each calendar month. To maximize your interest earnings, always deposit your monthly contribution on or before the 5th day of the month. Depositing after the 5th means you forfeit interest on that contribution for the entire calendar month.

The EEE Tax Advantage: Triple Tax-Free Wealth Accumulation

PPF enjoys the coveted 'Exempt-Exempt-Exempt' (EEE) tax status under the Old Tax Regime in India: annual deposits up to ₹1.5 Lakh qualify for deduction under Section 80C, annual accrued interest is 100% tax-exempt, and the final maturity corpus withdrawn after 15 years is completely free of income tax and capital gains tax.

Account Extension Rules: 5-Year Blocks With and Without Contributions

Upon completing the mandatory 15-year lock-in period, PPF account holders can extend their account indefinitely in blocks of 5 years. You can extend with continued fresh contributions (by submitting Form H within one year of maturity) to keep building tax-free wealth, or extend without contributions while your full accumulated corpus continues earning sovereign interest.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the current PPF interest rate and how is it calculated?

The Government of India notifies the PPF interest rate quarterly (currently benchmarked at 7.1% per annum). Interest is calculated on the lowest balance between the close of the 5th day and the last day of each calendar month, and credited annually on March 31st.

What makes the Public Provident Fund (PPF) an EEE tax-saving instrument?

PPF enjoys sovereign 'Exempt-Exempt-Exempt' (EEE) tax status under the Old Tax Regime: contributions qualify for deduction under Section 80C up to ₹1.5 Lakh per year, accumulated annual interest is 100% tax-exempt, and the final maturity proceeds are completely exempt from income tax.

Can I extend my PPF account after the mandatory 15-year maturity?

Yes. Upon completing the initial 15-year tenure, you can extend your PPF account indefinitely in blocks of 5 years. You may choose to extend with continued fresh contributions (submitting Form H within one year of maturity) or without any further contributions while continuing to earn interest.

Why is it crucial to deposit PPF funds on or before the 5th of each month?

Because interest is computed on the lowest balance between the 5th and the end of the month, depositing on or before the 5th ensures your new contribution earns interest for that entire month. Depositing after the 5th causes you to forfeit interest on that instalment for the month.
