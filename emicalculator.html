How this advanced EMI calculator works

This tool simulates your loan month-by-month using amortization logic. It calculates EMI, splits each payment into principal and interest, and tracks outstanding balance over time.


You can model one-time or annual prepayments, choose whether prepayment reduces tenure or EMI, and test floating-rate changes at a chosen month for realistic repayment planning.

EMI Planning in Practice

Effective loan planning combines affordability with cost optimization. A lower EMI may feel comfortable monthly, but tenure and total interest can increase significantly.


Use Prepayment Strategically

Prepaying in the initial years often yields the largest interest savings. Even moderate annual prepayments can reduce tenure materially.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is EMI?

EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) is the fixed monthly payment toward your loan, consisting of both principal and interest. The split between the two shifts over the tenure.

Why is early EMI mostly interest?

Interest is charged on the outstanding principal, which is highest at the start of the loan. So in the early months, a larger share of each EMI goes toward interest rather than principal repayment.

Does prepayment always help?

Generally yes, especially early in the tenure when the outstanding principal is large. Prepaying early reduces future interest substantially; most Indian home loans do not charge a prepayment penalty on floating-rate loans.

Reduce EMI or reduce tenure — which is better?

Reducing tenure usually saves the most total interest, while reducing EMI improves monthly cash flow. Choose tenure reduction if you can afford the same EMI and want the loan paid off sooner.

Can floating-rate changes increase total cost?

Yes. When lending rates rise, your lender may either increase the EMI or extend the tenure to keep the EMI same. Extending tenure means you pay more interest over time.

Should I include processing fee in planning?

Yes. Processing fees, insurance add-ons, and documentation charges are typically 0.5%–2% of the loan amount and should be included when comparing two loan offers on total cost.

How is EMI calculated?

EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1), where P is the loan principal, r the monthly interest rate (annual ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n the number of monthly installments.

What is the EMI for a ₹10 lakh loan at 10% for 10 years?

At a 10% annual rate for 120 months, the EMI is approximately ₹13,215. Total repayment over 10 years is around ₹15.86 lakh, of which about ₹5.86 lakh goes to interest.

What is the EMI for a ₹50 lakh home loan at 8.5% for 30 years?

At an 8.5% annual rate for 360 months, the monthly EMI is approximately ₹38,446. Over 30 years the total interest exceeds the principal — long tenures significantly raise the total cost.

What is the EMI for a ₹5 lakh car loan at 9% for 5 years?

For ₹5 lakh at 9% annual rate for 60 months, the EMI is approximately ₹10,376. Total repayment is around ₹6.22 lakh, with about ₹1.22 lakh in interest.

How does loan tenure affect EMI?

A longer tenure lowers the monthly EMI but raises the total interest paid. A shorter tenure costs more per month but dramatically reduces the total interest burden.

What is an amortization schedule?

It is a year-by-year (or month-by-month) breakdown showing how much of each EMI goes to interest versus principal, and the remaining balance after each payment. This calculator produces it automatically.

Can I get a loan if my EMI exceeds 50% of income?

Most Indian lenders prefer a debt-to-income ratio under 50% — ideally below 40%. A higher ratio increases rejection risk or requires a longer tenure/smaller loan to fit the criteria.

What is the difference between flat and reducing balance rate?

Flat-rate loans compute interest on the original principal throughout, making the effective rate nearly double the quoted rate. Reducing-balance loans (standard in India) compute interest on the declining outstanding amount — this is what this calculator uses.

Does this calculator account for prepayment?

Yes. Use the prepayment field to add an extra lump-sum payment at any point — the simulator recalculates the amortization schedule and shows interest saved.

Rules and Regulations

  • Indian home-loan rates in 2026 typically fall between 8.5% and 10.5%, driven by the RBI repo rate plus bank spreads.
  • Floating-rate home loans carry zero prepayment penalty under RBI guidance; fixed-rate loans may charge 2–4%.
  • Home-loan interest qualifies for a tax deduction of up to ₹2 lakh per year under Section 24(b) for a self-occupied property.
  • Lenders generally keep total monthly EMIs (including all existing loans) under 50% of net monthly income.

Use Cases and Practical Tips

Compare EMIs across home, car, education, and personal loans before you borrow. Indian lenders prefer total monthly EMIs to stay below 35–40% of net salary — treat that ratio as your red line while sizing the loan, and test shorter tenures to see how much interest you can save.

Common Mistakes

  • Treating a flat-rate quote as a reducing-balance quote, or vice versa — the effective rates differ by nearly 2×.
  • Leaving processing fees, insurance, and documentation charges out of the total-cost comparison.
  • Stretching the tenure to the maximum available instead of picking the shortest one you can comfortably afford.
