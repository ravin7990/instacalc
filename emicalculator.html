How this advanced EMI calculator works

This tool simulates your loan month-by-month using amortization logic. It calculates EMI, splits each payment into principal and interest, and tracks outstanding balance over time.


You can model one-time or annual prepayments, choose whether prepayment reduces tenure or EMI, and test floating-rate changes at a chosen month for realistic repayment planning.


Core intents covered: EMI calculation, amortization schedule, prepayment impact, and total borrowing cost analysis.

What is EMI?

EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) is the fixed monthly payment toward your loan, consisting of both principal and interest.

Why is early EMI mostly interest?

Interest is calculated on outstanding principal. Since principal is highest at the beginning, interest share is larger in early payments.

Does prepayment always help?

Generally yes, especially early in the tenure. It reduces principal and lowers future interest burden.

Reduce EMI or reduce tenure: which is better?

Reducing tenure usually saves more total interest, while reducing EMI improves monthly cash flow. Choose based on your priorities.

Can floating-rate changes increase total cost?

Yes. A rate increase can raise EMI or extend tenure, depending on lender terms and your repayment strategy.

Should I include processing fee in planning?

Yes. Fees and upfront costs materially affect actual borrowing expense and should be included in total loan cost comparisons.

EMI Planning in Practice

Effective loan planning combines affordability with cost optimization. A lower EMI may feel comfortable monthly, but tenure and total interest can increase significantly.


Use Prepayment Strategically

Prepaying in the initial years often yields the largest interest savings. Even moderate annual prepayments can reduce tenure materially.


Track Real Cost, Not Just EMI

Compare options based on total repayment, fees, and payoff time. This gives a complete picture of loan efficiency before choosing a repayment strategy.
